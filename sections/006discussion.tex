\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We studied neural posterior estimation as an amortized approach to Bayesian
dynamic borrowing: pretrain once on a broad simulation distribution, then obtain
approximate posteriors for new current--external pairs in a single forward pass.
The simulation study is the primary methodological evaluation because it
isolates covariate shift, outcome drift, and their combination; ADNI is a
complementary real-data illustration showing that cohort mismatch can pull
inference away from the concurrent target even when discrimination remains
strong. The empirical scope is a scalar current-study target---an additive
study-effect parameter in the Gaussian simulations and a cohort-level risk
parameter in ADNI. Covariate effects, treatment--covariate interactions, and
other causal functionals require additional evaluation; Appendix~\ref{app:covariate-effect}
provides only a limited illustrative check for one target-aware covariate-effect
analysis.

Two limitations are worth emphasizing. Posterior quality depends on the
pretraining simulator, so mismatch patterns outside its support cannot be
handled reliably. The current implementation also uses hand-crafted summaries:
these expose auditable signals of covariate shift, outcome-mechanism drift,
overlap, and joint mismatch, but may miss structure that richer patient-level
representations could learn. Runnable code is included in the supplementary
material; ADNI raw data require separate data-use access. Future work will study
richer paired-data representations, posterior-spread calibration, multi-source
borrowing, and broader evaluation across estimands and prospective settings.
