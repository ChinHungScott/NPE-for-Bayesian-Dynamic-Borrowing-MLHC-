\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We studied neural posterior estimation as an amortized approach to Bayesian
dynamic borrowing. The basic appeal of the method is straightforward: instead
of refitting a borrowing model from scratch for each new current--external data
pair, we train the posterior estimator once on a broad simulation distribution
and then use it to produce approximate posterior inference by a single forward
pass.

The simulation study is the main basis for methodological evaluation, since it
allows us to examine behavior under controlled forms of covariate shift,
outcome drift, and their combination. The ADNI analysis plays a different role.
It shows, in a real dataset, that substantial cohort mismatch can arise in
practice and that direct borrowing can pull inference away from the concurrent
target even when discrimination remains strong.

Several limitations should be kept in mind. First, the quality of NPE depends
on the simulator used for training. If the training distribution does not cover
the kinds of mismatch likely to appear in practice, the resulting posterior
approximation may not be reliable. Second, the present implementation uses
summary-based inputs. This keeps the method simple and computationally
manageable, but it also means that the quality of the posterior depends in part
on the quality of the summaries.

Another point is that the ADNI section should be interpreted as an illustration
rather than a definitive benchmark. The target in that analysis is a
cohort-specific risk parameter, not a randomized treatment effect. Its value in
the paper is that it shows the borrowing problem in a real-data setting and
makes the consequences of naive borrowing easy to see.

There are several directions for future work. One is to study whether richer
representations of paired datasets can improve posterior behavior in more
complex settings. For example, transformer-based patient encoders could be used
to summarize irregular longitudinal records before posterior estimation, rather
than relying only on hand-crafted summaries. Another is to investigate
calibration adjustments for the posterior spread, especially in the more
difficult mismatch regimes. A third is to consider settings with multiple
candidate external sources, where the main challenge is not only how much to
borrow, but also from which source to borrow.
