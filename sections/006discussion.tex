\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We studied neural posterior estimation as an amortized approach to
Bayesian dynamic borrowing: pretrain the posterior estimator once on a
broad simulation distribution, then obtain approximate posteriors for
new current--external pairs in a single forward pass.
The simulation study is the primary basis for methodological
evaluation, since it isolates behavior under controlled covariate
shift, outcome drift, and their combination. The ADNI analysis plays a
complementary role: it shows that substantial cohort mismatch arises in
real data and that direct borrowing can pull inference away from the
concurrent target even when discrimination remains strong. ADNI is an
illustration rather than a benchmark---the target is a cohort-specific
risk parameter, not a randomized treatment effect.

Two limitations are worth emphasizing. The quality of the approximate
posterior depends on the pretraining simulator; mismatch patterns not
covered by the simulator cannot be expected to be handled reliably.
And the current implementation consumes hand-crafted summaries, so
posterior quality inherits the sufficiency (or insufficiency) of those
summaries for the mismatch at hand.

Three directions extend the work naturally: richer paired-data
representations (e.g., transformer-based patient encoders for irregular
longitudinal records) to reduce reliance on hand-crafted summaries;
calibration adjustments for posterior spread in harder mismatch
regimes; and the multi-source setting, where the question is not only
how much to borrow but from which source.
