\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We studied neural posterior estimation as an amortized approach to
Bayesian dynamic borrowing: pretrain once on a broad simulation
distribution, then obtain approximate posteriors for new
current--external pairs in a single forward pass. The simulation study
is the primary methodological evaluation because it isolates covariate
shift, outcome drift, and their combination. ADNI is complementary: it
shows that cohort mismatch arises in real data and that direct borrowing
can pull inference away from the concurrent target even when
discrimination remains strong.
The empirical scope is a scalar current-study target---an additive
study-effect parameter in the Gaussian simulations and a cohort-level
risk parameter in ADNI. Covariate effects, treatment--covariate
interactions, and other causal functionals require additional evaluation;
Appendix~\ref{app:covariate-effect} provides only a limited illustrative
check for one target-aware covariate-effect analysis. This framing keeps
the real-data example aligned with the estimand actually analyzed rather
than treating ADNI as a randomized treatment-effect study.

Two limitations are worth emphasizing. Posterior quality depends on the
pretraining simulator, so mismatch patterns outside its support cannot be
handled reliably. The current implementation also uses hand-crafted
summaries: these expose auditable signals of covariate shift,
outcome-mechanism drift, overlap, and joint mismatch, but may miss
structure that a richer patient-level representation could learn.
Runnable code is included in the supplementary material; ADNI raw data
require separate data-use access.

Future work will study richer paired-data representations, posterior-spread
calibration, and multi-source borrowing. A broader evaluation across
estimands and prospective data-borrowing settings would further clarify
when amortized borrowing is most useful in practice.
