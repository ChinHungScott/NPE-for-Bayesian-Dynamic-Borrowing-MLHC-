\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setup and inferential target}

Let $\mathcal{D}_c = \{(x_{ci}, y_{ci})\}_{i=1}^{n_c}$ and
$\mathcal{D}_e = \{(x_{ej}, y_{ej})\}_{j=1}^{n_e}$ be the concurrent and
external datasets. Inference targets a scalar treatment effect $\theta$
\emph{defined on the concurrent population}; the external sample is
used only insofar as it is compatible with this population. The classical
Bayesian approach is to specify a dynamic-borrowing prior and draw samples
from the joint posterior $p(\theta, \phi \mid \mathcal{D}_c, \mathcal{D}_e)$,
where $\phi$ is a borrowing-strength parameter (e.g., the power-prior
discount $a_0$).

\subsection{Amortized posterior inference}

We replace per-dataset posterior computation with a single pretrained
network. Concretely, we learn an \emph{amortized} conditional posterior
\begin{equation}
  q_{\phi}\!\big(\theta \mid s(\mathcal{D}_c, \mathcal{D}_e)\big)
  \;\approx\;
  p\!\big(\theta \mid \mathcal{D}_c, \mathcal{D}_e\big),
  \label{eq:amortized}
\end{equation}
where $s(\cdot,\cdot) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is a fixed-length summary statistic
and $\phi$ denotes network parameters. Given a prior $\pi(\theta, \psi)$
over the target and nuisance parameters, and a simulator
$(\theta, \psi) \mapsto (\mathcal{D}_c, \mathcal{D}_e)$, we generate
$M$ training triples $(\theta^{(m)}, \mathcal{D}_c^{(m)}, \mathcal{D}_e^{(m)})$
and fit $\phi$ once by amortized variational inference. At deployment, posterior
inference for a new pair reduces to evaluating $s$ and performing one
forward pass through $q_\phi$---an $O(1)$ operation in the sample size.

We use a Gaussian posterior family,
\begin{equation}
  q_{\phi}(\theta \mid s) = \mathcal{N}\!\big(m_{\phi}(s),\, \sigma_{\phi}^2(s)\big),
  \label{eq:gaussian-head}
\end{equation}
trained by minimizing the expected negative log-likelihood
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\phi}
  \;=\;
  \arg\min_{\phi}\,
  \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}
    -\log q_{\phi}\!\big(\theta^{(m)} \,\big|\, s(\mathcal{D}_c^{(m)}, \mathcal{D}_e^{(m)})\big),
  \label{eq:training-obj}
\end{equation}
which is the standard NPE objective and is minimized, in the
well-specified limit $M \to \infty$, by the true posterior $p(\theta \mid s)$.

\subsection{Summary statistics}
\label{sec:summary-stats}

The choice of $s(\mathcal{D}_c, \mathcal{D}_e)$ controls the sufficient
statistics seen by the network. We use a 55-dimensional summary composed of
four blocks, each designed to expose a different aspect of source
(in)compatibility:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Marginal moments.} Per-source means and standard deviations
    of $X$ and $Y$, and per-source sample sizes $(n_c, n_e)$.
  \item \textbf{Conditional structure.} OLS coefficients
    $\hat{\beta}_c, \hat{\beta}_e$ and residual variances from fitting
    $Y \sim X$ separately in each source; the gap
    $\Delta_\beta = \hat\beta_c - \hat\beta_e$ encodes outcome-mechanism
    drift.
  \item \textbf{Covariate overlap.} Propensity-score summaries based on
    $e(x) = P(S{=}1 \mid X{=}x)$, where $S{=}1$ indexes the concurrent
    source. External observations receive overlap weights
    $w_j = 2\sqrt{e(x_{ej})\{1 - e(x_{ej})\}}$, which yield a weighted
    mean, effective sample size $n_e^{\mathrm{eff}} = (\sum w_j)^2 / \sum w_j^2$,
    and a weight concentration score.
  \item \textbf{Joint-shift diagnostic.} Per-covariate mean gap
    $\Delta_\mu = \bar{x}_c - \bar{x}_e$ and the scalar Wasserstein-1
    distance between the empirical covariate distributions.
\end{enumerate}
This block-structured design makes the summary \emph{exchangeability-aware}:
Block 1 detects marginal shift, Block 2 detects outcome drift, Block 3
quantifies propensity-score overlap, and Block 4 captures joint mismatch
that neither marginal nor conditional summaries alone would resolve.

\subsection{Training distribution and regimes}
\label{sec:regimes}

The amortized posterior in \eqref{eq:amortized} is only as useful as the
distribution over $(\mathcal{D}_c, \mathcal{D}_e)$ it was trained on. We
therefore construct the simulator to span a taxonomy of
source-mismatch regimes defined on two axes---covariate exchangeability
and outcome exchangeability---yielding six scenarios (SC1--SC6) summarized
in Table~\ref{tab:regimes}.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{cll}
\toprule
Scenario & Covariates & Outcomes \\
\midrule
SC1 & exchangeable        & exchangeable \\
SC2 & partial shift       & exchangeable \\
SC3 & exchangeable        & partial drift \\
SC4 & strong shift        & exchangeable \\
SC5 & exchangeable        & strong drift \\
SC6 & strong shift        & strong drift \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Source-mismatch regimes. Rows cross two axes: covariate
exchangeability ($\mu_e = \mu_c$ vs.\ shifted) and outcome exchangeability
($\delta_e = 0$ vs.\ nonzero). SC1 and SC6 are the extremes; SC2--SC5
isolate a single axis.}
\label{tab:regimes}
\end{table}

Each regime is instantiated by a Gaussian linear model:
\begin{align}
  X_{ci} &\sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_c \mathbf{1}_p,\, \Sigma), &
  X_{ej} &\sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_e \mathbf{1}_p,\, \Sigma), \\
  Y_{ci} &= X_{ci}^\top \beta + \theta + \varepsilon_{ci}, &
  Y_{ej} &= X_{ej}^\top \beta + \theta + \delta_e + \varepsilon_{ej},
\end{align}
with $\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$. Covariate shift is
controlled by $\mu_e - \mu_c$; outcome drift by $\delta_e$. The training
prior $\pi$ draws $(\mu_e - \mu_c, \delta_e)$ uniformly over a range
that includes all six regimes, so the learned $q_\phi$ is trained across
the full shift taxonomy rather than specialized to any single regime.
At evaluation time we fix $\theta = 1$ (power) or $\theta = 0$ (type-I
error) and measure performance within each SC1--SC6 regime separately.
